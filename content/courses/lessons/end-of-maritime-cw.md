# The end of an era at sea

For most of the twentieth century, every large ship carried a radio officer listening for Morse. That ended with the **Global Maritime Distress and Safety System (GMDSS)**, which became fully mandatory on **1 February 1999**. Satellites and digital calling replaced the wireless room, and coast stations around the world closed their Morse watches.

Morse didn't disappear, though. Amateur radio operators kept it alive and still use it every day. It gets through when voice can't, needs very simple equipment, and is, quite simply, a pleasure to use. Most countries, including the UK in **2003**, stopped requiring it for a licence, but plenty of people still choose to learn it.
