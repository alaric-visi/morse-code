# One code for the world

American Morse was awkward on long lines and undersea cables, where the internal spaces were easy to lose. In **1848** Friedrich Gerke simplified it for the German telegraphs: no internal spaces, one length of dash, and several letters changed.

His version, with a few more changes, was adopted at the **1865 International Telegraph Conference** in Paris. That **International Morse Code** is the one you're learning; American Morse was mostly confined to North American landlines and railways.
